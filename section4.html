See It for Yourself

10 min

By the end of this section, you'll have investigated real tree equity data in a Canadian city — and connected what you see to the concepts and benchmarks you've already learned.

Three Lenses

Before you explore

The Nature Canada report proposes three ways to think about tree equity in any community. Keep these lenses in mind as you investigate — they'll shape what you notice and what you look for.

📍
Proximity
Flip to explore →
The question it asks
How close are trees to where people actually live and work?
Look for gaps between neighborhoods. Is green space evenly distributed, or concentrated in certain areas?
🌿
Quality
Flip to explore →
The question it asks
Are the trees healthy? Is the urban forest biodiverse and well-maintained?
Not all tree coverage is equal. A few mature trees provide more cooling and air quality benefits than many small saplings.
🗣️
Participation
Flip to explore →
The question it asks
Do community members have a meaningful voice in decisions about green spaces?
Data can show the gap — but community voice determines whether anything changes. Who gets consulted, and who doesn't?
