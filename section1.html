Why Your Neighborhood Matters
~8 minutes
By the end of this section, you'll start noticing something you may have always seen — but never named.

Think about the neighborhood where you grew up. Was it green and shady, or mostly concrete and pavement? Could you walk to a park, or was the nearest green space a long drive away?

These details may seem small, but they have real consequences for health, well-being, and resilience to climate change. And they are not distributed equally.

Watch Leandro's story about growing up in Santo Domingo and then moving to Providence, Rhode Island. Pay attention to what he noticed about the differences between neighborhoods.
Title: Tree Equity Score Creator: American Forests Length: ~5 minutes
This is not a coincidence

What Leandro noticed in Providence is not unique to Rhode Island. The same patterns appear in Vancouver, Victoria, Calgary, and Toronto. In each city, low-income and racialized neighborhoods consistently have less tree cover, more pavement, and fewer green spaces.

This is not accidental. It is the result of decisions — which means it can be changed.
