What the Research Confirms

These aren't just abstract concepts. In Section 1, you noticed something. Now here's what researchers found when they measured it across five Canadian cities.

Nature Canada, 2022 — Urban Forests and Tree Equity in Canadian Cities

Key Findings
Consistently Less
Low-income neighborhoods have measurably lower tree canopy across all five cities studied — Vancouver, Abbotsford, Calgary, Toronto, and Montreal.
Greater Heat Exposure
Racialized communities face disproportionate urban heat island effects — higher temperatures, more pavement, fewer cooling spaces — than wealthier, whiter neighborhoods.
By Design, Not Chance
Three causes: funding gaps in underserved areas, planning processes that exclude trees, and weak community engagement. These patterns reflect decades of policy decisions.

The Benchmark

The 3-30-300 Rule

Researchers proposed a simple benchmark for tree equity — three numbers that every neighborhood should meet. How does yours compare to what you just estimated?

3
trees visible
Flip to explore →
What it means
You should be able to see at least 3 trees from your home or the entrance to your building.
Why this number
Visible trees reduce stress, improve mood, and create connection to nature. If you can't see a single tree, the psychological and physical benefits of urban green space don't reach you.
30%
canopy cover
Flip to explore →
What it means
At least 30% of your neighborhood's surface area should be covered by tree canopy when viewed from above.
Why this number
At 30% canopy, neighborhoods experience measurable cooling effects, reduced stormwater runoff, and better air quality. Below that threshold, urban heat islands intensify — especially in summer.
300m
to green space
Flip to explore →
What it means
Everyone should live within 300 metres — roughly a 3–4 minute walk — of a quality green space.
Why this number
300 metres is the distance at which people actually use green spaces regularly. Beyond that, visit frequency drops sharply. Access on paper doesn't translate to access in practice.
